## Key points

- Firms file data the ministry uses to pick inspection targets.
- First annual scoring on a new risk scale is due Feb. 1.
- Law ties audits to international standards; no recognition order found.
- Ministry has not said whether or when the new version is mandatory.

Uzbekistan's finance ministry has updated "Audit", the online system audit firms use to file data the ministry draws on to choose inspection targets.

[The Ministry of Economy and Finance said](https://gov.uz/oz/imv/news/view/250401) on Nov. 20 that the system, launched in 2019, had been improved to meet current requirements; it gave the address as audit.imv.uz.

The update comes weeks before the risk analysis due by Feb. 1, 2027, which would be the first annual one under a new 100-point scale. Firms file their annual information by Jan. 20, on paper or through the system.

[The ministry counted](https://gov.uz/oz/imv/news/view/162515) 176 audit firms and 1,337 holders of an auditor's certificate in the first quarter of 2026.

Below is how quality control works, where the platform fits and how other markets compare.

## Which standards apply?

Article 9 of the [Law on Auditing Activity](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/5307899) makes the standards of the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board the standards of auditing in Uzbekistan; article 29 obliges every firm to follow them.

They apply if they do not contradict Uzbek law and are posted on the regulator's website with permission from the International Federation of Accountants (IFAC).

[Cabinet resolution No. 171](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/5952173) of April 11, 2022, ends the recognition procedure with an order of the minister, which need not be registered with the Justice Ministry.

No such order turned up on Nov. 27 on the national legal database or on the ministry's list of audit acts. The ministry reported in May that all 50 auditing standards had been translated into Uzbek and the final texts were being checked.

Its [standards page](https://gov.uz/oz/imv/sections/view/22099) offers English originals and a draft Uzbek translation dated 2023 and 2024. Article 48 of the law already lists compliance with the standards among the measures of audit quality.

## What standards require

International Standard on Quality Management 1 (ISQM 1), effective under IAASB rules from Dec. 15, 2022, requires each firm to run a risk-based quality system.

"The firm shall design and implement a risk assessment process to establish quality objectives, identify and assess quality risks and design and implement responses to address the quality risks," the standard says.

"External inspections are not a substitute for the firm's internal monitoring activities," the standard says, a point that weighs more where low-risk firms are not inspected at all.

## How does control work?

The law makes internal quality control a condition of providing audit services. External control is run by the state body, in practice the ministry, with national associations of auditors.

Checks follow a risk analysis made every year by Feb. 1, or on a complaint, and last no more than 10 working days. [Resolution No. 538](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/6212685) of Sept. 28, 2022, applies them to firms that did statutory audits in the past two reporting periods.

[Order No. 377](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8306546), published on July 3 and in force three months later, scores warning signs out of 100 points in five groups of 20.

The groups cover violations, staff and qualifications, services and reporting, anti-money laundering controls and internal control. Missing the Jan. 20 filing adds 5 points; having no internal quality control system adds 3.

A separate electronic system, "Risk analysis", sets the level automatically. The order puts high risk at 81 to 100 points, medium at 61 to 81 and low at up to 61, so 81 falls in two bands.

The ministry may not request documents during the analysis, and a risk level alone is not grounds for sanctions. The table shows what follows from each level.

| Risk level | Points | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| High | 81-100 | Inspection, agreed with the business ombudsman, if violations are not fixed |
| Medium | 61-81 | Warning letter; up to a month to fix; then treated as high risk |
| Low | up to 61 | No inspection |

*Source: Order No. 377 of the Ministry of Economy and Finance; Cabinet resolution No. 538*

In an illustrative case, a firm scoring 70 points would get a warning and up to a month to put things right.

## Where the platform fits

Article 12 of the law has the ministry monitor firms remotely through "Audit". Resolution No. 538 and Order No. 377 list data filed through it among the sources for the risk analysis.

The yearly cycle below comes from the law, Resolution No. 538 and the ministry's [rules on annual reporting](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/2251569).

| When | Step | Through "Audit" |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 20 | Firm files annual information | On paper or online |
| Feb 1 | Ministry completes risk analysis | No, "Risk analysis" system |
| Feb 10 | Ministry tells each firm its risk level | Yes |
| 3 working days after the notice | Firm sends proof that violations are fixed | Yes |
| 10 days before an inspection | Ministry gives notice | Yes |

*Sources: Law on Auditing Activity, art. 47; Cabinet resolution No. 538, pp. 17, 18, 22; ministry rules reg. 2515, p. 2*

Most of the exchange before an inspection already runs through the system; the ministry says the update simplifies document flow and saves time.

## How others do it

In the United States, the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, overseen by the Securities and Exchange Commission, inspects firms that regularly audit more than 100 public companies every year and others at least every three years, under [U.S. law](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title15/html/USCODE-2023-title15-chap98-subchapI-sec7214.htm).

Its inspection reports are made public, but criticism of a firm's quality control stays private if the firm fixes it to the board's satisfaction within 12 months of the report.

In the European Union, oversight bodies run by non-practitioners inspect auditors of public-interest entities on a risk basis at least every three years, or six for small and medium-sized ones, under [Regulation 537/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02014R0537-20240109). Auditors of other companies that EU law requires to be audited are reviewed on a risk basis at least every six years under the [audit directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02006L0043-20260318).

Kazakhstan's [audit law](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/Z980000304_) makes quality control risk-based. Auditors of listed and financial companies, among others, are checked by a professional council whose board is chaired by a head or deputy head of the state regulator, with professional bodies holding at most a third of seats.

Under Kazakhstan's [standard rules](https://adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2100023080), a planned check covers no more than three years since the last one, and two grades of 2, the lowest on the scale, are grounds to expel a firm from its professional body.

In Uzbekistan, [IFAC's undated profile](https://www.ifac.org/about-ifac/membership/profile/uzbekistan) notes, there is no separate independent audit oversight authority distinct from the ministry.

## What is still open?

The Nov. 20 announcement did not say what changed from the 2019 version, whether firms must use the new one or from what date. No act on a switchover was found, and the portal says it is working in test mode.

On Nov. 27 the portal's registers listed 191 audit organisations and 1,449 certificate numbers, including test records and duplicates, so they do not match the ministry's count.

A 2018 [presidential resolution](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/3914504), with no mark of repeal, still calls for checks at least every three years, while the law ties them to risk.
