## Key points

- IFRS 1 requires three reconciliations: of equity at the transition date, of equity at the end of the last period under previous GAAP, and of total comprehensive income for that period.
- The reconciliations must explain every adjustment and separate corrections of errors from changes in accounting policy.
- The most frequent discrepancies arise in property, plant and equipment (revaluation and impairment), leases, deferred taxes and allowances for doubtful debts.
- If the reconciliations do not agree, the financial statements are not issued: a break is a defect in the transformation, not a "feature of the transition".

## Why the user needs the reconciliations

An investor or bank sees two sets of financial statements for the same year with different profit and different equity. The reconciliation explains the difference line by line: how much is due to different recognition of leases, how much to impairment, how much to deferred taxes. Without a reconciliation there is no trust in the new financial statements, and with an incorrect one even less.

## Where equity gets lost

**Property, plant and equipment.** Moving to historical cost or deemed cost at the transition date changes the depreciation base; impairment under IAS 36 is often recognised for the first time. A typical error is to adjust the balance sheet but not recalculate depreciation for the comparative period.

**Leases.** Under IFRS 16 the lessee recognises a right-of-use asset and a liability. At the transition date the difference between them goes to equity; in the comparative period lease expense is replaced by depreciation and interest, and profit changes.

**Deferred taxes.** Every adjustment to equity creates a temporary difference. Deferred tax is calculated on all adjustments at once, not selectively; otherwise the equity reconciliation and the profit reconciliation will disagree with each other.

**Provisions and estimated liabilities.** The allowance for doubtful debts under the expected credit loss model and the provisions under IAS 37 are recognised in a different amount from NAS, and sometimes for the first time.

## How we check the reconciliations

The transformation worksheet is built so that the sum of all adjustments for each item flows into the reconciliation automatically. Before release an integrity check is performed: adjustments sum to zero, equity at the end of the comparative period in the reconciliation equals equity in the statement of financial position, profit in the reconciliation equals profit in the statement of comprehensive income, and every adjustment is explained with a reference to the standard. Any break stops the release.
