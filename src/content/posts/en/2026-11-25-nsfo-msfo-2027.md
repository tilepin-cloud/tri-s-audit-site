## Key points

- A framework and three new standards take effect; old standards remain.
- No document says which prevails over binding old rules.
- Ministry's model chart of accounts is keyed mostly to unadopted standards.
- Standards arriving later may force companies to restate past figures.

Uzbek companies will keep their books under two sets of standards from Jan. 1, 2027, because the government is replacing old ones one at a time.

A [conceptual framework](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8261487) and three national financial reporting standards, known by their Russian initials NSFO, take effect that day: on presentation, accounting policies and agriculture.

The Ministry of Economy and Finance approved them in 2026 as standards [No. 1](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8405084), [2](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8130039) and [70](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8514080); a fourth, [No. 77](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/8379837) for non-profit organisations, follows on Jan. 1, 2028.

"The old one can be used too," Andrey Bayrashev, an IFRS expert at consultancy ElectEx, said of the old chart of accounts in a practitioners' Telegram group on Nov. 4, 2026.

The new standards apply to all legal entities except budget bodies and [public-interest entities](https://lex.uz/uz/docs/7959764); the latter must move to full International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS). The October 2026 register lists 449 such entities, mostly banks and other financial firms.

Of 23 areas of accounting compared for this analysis, the new documents cover four. Old national accounting standards, known as NSBU, still govern 14, including fixed assets, inventories, revenue and leases.

Three, including income tax, have no standard yet. The statutory forms keep their old layout, and non-profits get their own standard only in 2028.

A [presidential resolution](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7728927) of Sept. 15, 2025, ordered the ministry to propose national standards harmonised with the IFRS for small and medium-sized entities (SMEs).

A comparison of the texts for this analysis shows the adopted standards draw mainly on full IFRS instead, as Bayrashev also said in the group on Nov. 15.

## How the mix works

Paragraph 13 of NSFO No. 2 links the two systems. Where no new standard covers a transaction, management should consider the new standards "or national accounting standards in force" on similar matters.

The orders repeal only one old standard, NSBU No. 1 on financial statements, together with the 2024 conceptual framework. The ministry says a new standard does not cancel old documents automatically.

No adopted act or ministry guide says which prevails when a new standard conflicts with an old rule that remains binding, such as the chart of accounts or the statutory forms.

The forms show the clash. A ministry manual keeps the 2027 forms, whose lines are tied to accounts of the old chart, NSBU No. 21, and have no line for other comprehensive income.

NSFO No. 1 requires comprehensive income and five categories of income and expenses, and it covers statements filed with state bodies.

Vera Bell, managing partner of Crowe Uzbekistan, asked at a forum in May 2026 what an auditor would sign off on if a report followed the new standards only in part, [buxgalter.uz reported](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text215372_itogi_ii_respublikanskogo_foruma_finansovyh_specialistov_nsfo_nacionalnaya_sertifikaciya_profstandarty_islamskie_finansy).

## Which chart of accounts?

[NSBU No. 21](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7282737) has not been repealed, and the ministry itself calls it the chart in force.

The ministry's model chart for the new standards, dated Oct. 29, 2026, is a recommendation and not binding "until approved by a legislative act".

The model chart has 286 codes keyed to 29 standards. As of Nov. 6, 2026, four of these 29 had been adopted, nine were drafts and 16 had not been published.

The same codes mean different things in the two charts. Code 1000 is materials under NSBU No. 21 and fixed assets in the model chart.

The open question, Bayrashev said, is whether NSBU No. 21 will lose force on Jan. 1, 2027.

## Later standards

A [draft first-time adoption standard](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11895) offers a single transition with one opening balance sheet. It was No. 7 when put out for comment and is No. 5 in the version given the portal's internal status 'approved'.

The single transition covers only the standards in force at the end of the first reporting period. For a first report for 2027 with comparatives, the transition date is Jan. 1, 2026, ElectEx's Tatyana Nazarova said.

Standards adopted later come in through their own transition rules or, without them, retrospectively under NSFO No. 2.

Retrospective application means restating comparative figures. Where the effect on the start of the prior year is material, NSFO No. 1 requires a third balance sheet.

Standard No. 77 and eight thematic drafts could arrive that way. Four of them, covering [cash flows](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11897), [inventories](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11907), [foreign currency](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11900) and non-profits, have no transition rules of their own.

Two more, on [intangibles](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11902) and [revenue](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11939), apply retrospectively with relief, and two apply prospectively. The [draft on fixed assets](https://regulation.adliya.uz/project/11908) refers to the first-time adoption standard.

Neither the consultation texts nor their portal cards name an effective date, though [buxgalter.uz reported](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text218002_nsfo-marafon_9_proektov_standartov_vynesli_na_obshchestvennoe_obsujdenie) a planned start of Jan. 1, 2027.

Tax adds a second layer. [ElectEx said](https://t.me/electex_ifrs/948) taxable profit should be calculated from accounting profit with adjustments set out in the Tax Code, or the move to international standards would "once again lose all meaning".

[Article 76 of the Tax Code](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4674893) says tax accounting is based on accounting data unless the code provides otherwise, so changes in recognition and measurement can reach the tax base, [ElectEx said](https://t.me/electex_ifrs/987).

About 70% of participants at the same forum said they were not ready for the transition, the buxgalter.uz report said.

## The case for mixing

The mixed regime is written into the standards themselves, in paragraph 13 of NSFO No. 2, and the 2024 framework already sat alongside the old standards.

Russia has run a similar regime for years. It began replacing its accounting rules with IFRS-based standards one by one in 1998, and a [law in force since 2013](https://legalacts.ru/doc/402_FZ-o-buhgalterskom-uchete/glava-4/statja-30/) keeps the old rules until new ones arrive.

The [1998 programme](https://legalacts.ru/doc/postanovlenie-pravitelstva-rf-ot-06031998-n-283/) set two years to adopt standards covering the bulk of IFRS requirements. Under the [latest draft programme](https://minfin.gov.ru/ru/document?id_4=317733), the last standard would become mandatory in 2033.

All three Russian standards checked, [FSBU 5/2019](https://legalacts.ru/doc/prikaz-minfina-rossii-ot-15112019-n-180n-ob-utverzhdenii/), [6/2020](https://legalacts.ru/doc/prikaz-minfina-rossii-ot-17092020-n-204n-ob-utverzhdenii/) and [9/2025](https://legalacts.ru/doc/prikaz-minfina-rossii-ot-16052025-n-56n-ob-utverzhdenii/), carry their own transition rules, and some let companies skip restating comparatives.

## What comes next

Seven of the nine drafts, including the first-time adoption standard, were given the internal status 'approved' on the government's drafting portal between Nov. 19 and Nov. 24, 2026. None had been registered by the justice ministry by Nov. 24.

For a calendar-year company, standards in force by Dec. 31, 2027, would fall into the single transition, if the first-time adoption standard keeps its consultation wording.
