## Key points

- The auditor must explicitly assess the risk of fraud at the financial statement level and at the assertion level, and document the response to each identified risk.
- Inquiries of those charged with governance about fraud become a separate, documented step: the board of directors will answer the auditor's questions, not only management.
- The auditor's report includes separate information about fraud where it is a key audit matter.
- For the client this means more inquiries about controls, journal entries and unusual transactions, and earlier than before.

## What exactly changes

The revised standard clarifies that professional scepticism is not an abstract requirement but a set of actions: a discussion within the team before work begins, inquiries of management and those charged with governance, analysis of journal entries for the whole period rather than only at the reporting date, and an explicit conclusion on how the auditor responded to each risk.

We already test complete populations of entries: ten journal entry tests with Benford distribution analysis and risk scores. The new requirements will therefore not come as a surprise to our clients. What will change is the volume of documentation and the list of questions for the board of directors.

> The risk of fraud is assessed on every engagement, regardless of whether there were findings in the past. The absence of findings is not evidence of the absence of risk.

## How to prepare

| Step | Who | When |
|---|---|---|
| Describe controls over manual entries and access rights in the accounting system | Chief accountant, IT | by the end of Q1 2027 |
| Appoint a person responsible for answering the auditor's questions on fraud at board level | Board of directors | when the audit plan is approved |
| Review the policy on conflicts of interest and related parties | Legal counsel | in 2026 |
| Close periods in the accounting system and record back-dated entries | Chief accountant | from the start of 2027 |

## What this means for cost and timing

The additional procedures increase the work at the planning and completion stages. For companies with closed periods and working controls over manual entries the increase is small; where periods are not closed and entries are edited retrospectively, the auditor will have to do more, and this will be reflected in the budget. The best preparation is order in the accounting system throughout 2026.
